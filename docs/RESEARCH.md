# Research and coverage notes

Initial research: 17 October 2026; expanded on 24 October 2026 in [Expanded coverage](EXPANDED-COVERAGE.md). This document distinguishes source inspection from browser testing. It does **not** claim that the extension has been tested on every deployment of any vendor, or that a successful UI click proves that a website stops tracking.

## Objective and limits

The extension expresses the user's rejection of optional cookies through the consent interface exposed by the website. Necessary cookies, including session/login cookies and the cookie recording refusal, may remain. A browser content script cannot guarantee that every website honors consent, remove all server-side tracking, or prevent cookies already set before a banner appears. Cookie removal and consent rejection are different operations.

Coverage combines explicit provider selectors with exact multilingual action labels inside a qualified cookie interface. The original 19 provider records listed below are expanded to 34 in version 2.1.0. CookieYes and OrestBida have separate modern and legacy records; Osano's hosted CMP and original open-source CookieConsent have different interfaces. These are compatibility rules, not measured success percentages.

## Method

Provider rules were independently written after inspecting official vendor documentation, vendor-served JavaScript, and upstream CMP source. Only selector names and action semantics were extracted. No third-party automatic-consent rule corpus was imported, and no vendor script is bundled or fetched by the extension. The research assets used for inspection are outside the extension directory.

Rules are intentionally scoped to identifiable provider interfaces. A reject selector must refer to a refusal or necessary-only action. A settings or save selector cannot by itself establish rejection. The engine must first determine that optional controls can safely be switched off before saving their selection. Close buttons, positional selectors, CSS colors, and generic secondary buttons do not establish refusal.

## Language and classification policy

The vocabulary contains explicit action phrases in 30+ languages, including English, German, French, Spanish, Italian, Portuguese, Dutch, Polish, Czech, Slovak, Danish, Swedish, Norwegian, Finnish, Greek, Romanian, Hungarian, Bulgarian, Croatian, Slovenian, Estonian, Latvian, Lithuanian, Turkish, Russian, Ukrainian, Arabic, Hebrew, Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Hindi, Indonesian and Vietnamese. This is a phrase collection, not a professionally audited translation pack or a claim of full language coverage.

Action matching is exact after Unicode NFKC normalization, case normalization, whitespace cleanup, directional-mark cleanup and trailing-punctuation removal. This reduces substring errors such as confusing “accept only necessary” with “accept all”. Explicit necessary-only phrases are refusal actions. Generic category words and optional category words are exposed separately. Cookie context uses explicit cookie/tracking terminology; a bare reference to consent, privacy, or data processing cannot qualify an unrelated dialog such as medical consent.

Provider rules handle configured text independently of these phrases when the selector has explicit semantics. Unknown wording, spelling errors, combined action labels, and custom interfaces may be skipped. Adding an English substring such as `no`, `reject`, or `settings` to a page-wide click search is not a safe language fallback.

## Rejected shortcuts and uncovered interfaces

- **Close is not refusal.** OneTrust, iubenda, Cookie Notice and other configurations demonstrate materially different close semantics. The extension must not hide or delete banners as a substitute for submitting a choice.
- **Button position/style is not semantics.** No `:first-child`, generic secondary-button selector, or button-color heuristic determines refusal. Quantcast layouts can use secondary buttons for different actions, so no broad secondary selector was included.
- **Sourcepoint action classes must be verified.** Version 2.1.0 uses the vendor's documented rejection classes `sp_choice_type_13` and `sp_choice_type_REJECT_ALL`. `sp_choice_type_11` is acceptance. Sourcepoint choices use only verified action selectors: custom JavaScript, dismissal, subscription and legitimate-interest-only choices are not guessed from labels. See [the expansion evidence](EXPANDED-COVERAGE.md).
- **Other CMPs are not automatically covered by name.** Quantcast, older Borlabs, new Usercentrics templates and heavily customized CMPs still require matching source-verified selectors or the conservative language fallback. The expansion adds specific TrustArc, Termly and CookieScript interfaces, not every generation of those vendors.
- **A disabled or ambiguous interface is left to the user.** Accept-only banners, pay-or-consent flows, browser-internal pages, inaccessible frames, closed shadow roots, and interfaces requiring trusted physical input may remain. The extension does not bypass paywalls or purchase alternatives.
- **Necessary-only consent still permits necessary cookies.** The browser does not provide a universal way to determine whether every website's category assignment is honest or technically accurate.

## Browser architecture evidence

[Chrome content-script documentation](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/extensions/develop/concepts/content-scripts) establishes that content scripts can inspect and operate the DOM, normally execute in an isolated JavaScript world, and can be declared in matching frames. Page globals such as `OneTrust` or `Didomi` are therefore not a universal direct content-script API. This implementation operates visible UI controls rather than injecting vendor code or calling arbitrary page functions.

[Chrome storage documentation](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/extensions/reference/api/storage) describes persistent extension settings and change events. Extension-local preferences and counts can be stored without uploading browsing data. Site choices remain the responsibility of each site's CMP.

[MDN's shadow-root documentation](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Element/attachShadow) distinguishes open and closed roots. Existing open roots can be traversed; an existing closed root is not exposed through `element.shadowRoot`. In Chrome, the extension feature-detects [`chrome.dom.openOrClosedShadowRoot`](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/extensions/reference/api/dom) to reach supported closed roots without injecting code into the page. Separate observers watch discovered roots; periodic discovery handles roots attached later to an existing host. Browser restrictions can still leave interfaces inaccessible.

The [service-worker lifecycle documentation](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/extensions/develop/concepts/service-workers/lifecycle) explains why global variables cannot durably hold tab counts. Session storage preserves those counts across worker restarts; a serialized update queue prevents concurrent frame messages from overwriting each other's increments. [Programmatic clicks are untrusted](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Event/isTrusted), so websites can refuse them. Counts therefore describe attempts rather than verified consent or cookie outcomes.

The [IAB TCF CMP API specification](https://github.com/InteractiveAdvertisingBureau/GDPR-Transparency-and-Consent-Framework/blob/master/TCFv2/IAB%20Tech%20Lab%20-%20CMP%20API%20v2.md) defines interoperability commands and consent-state events. It does not define a universal public “reject all optional cookies on this website” command usable across every CMP. Writing a fabricated consent string or calling an undocumented setter would not provide reliable rejection.

[Global Privacy Control](https://www.w3.org/TR/gpc/), browser cookie blocking, tracker blocking and this extension's visible consent actions are different mechanisms. No GPC signal is fabricated by these UI rules, and clicking a refusal is not a substitute for browser-level cookie controls. The extension's counters describe UI actions, not a network audit.

## Validation interpretation and maintenance

### AtkinsRéalis careers verification, 21 October 2026

The public [Systems Engineering Industrial Year Placement page](https://careers.atkinsrealis.com/en/jobs/systems-engineering-industrial-year-placement-pl-192) uses OneTrust. Its [English consent configuration](https://cdn.cookielaw.org/consent/a43f3bf8-cde3-4228-8962-4b9de2c54c71/019ed5be-2e96-7494-9b41-36f04ffa3a5b/en.json) disables both banner and preference-center Reject All buttons. The working route is **Cookies settings → optional categories off → Confirm my choices**.

Version 2.0.0 opened preferences but did not save because hidden Cookie List templates contained four unclassified checkbox placeholders. Version 2.0.1 ignores only the reviewed native unchecked, nonmixed templates inside OneTrust's hidden `#ot-pc-lst`, with hidden associated labels and no category/purpose metadata. Checked, mixed, visible, partially selected, or unfamiliar controls still prevent saving. The [vendor SDK 202609.2.0](https://cdn.cookielaw.org/scripttemplates/202609.2.0/otBannerSdk.js) was inspected: select-all controls can change consent, so the extension never clicks these templates or treats them as proof of vendor refusal. It also distinguishes explicit category names from explanatory paragraphs that say “If you do not allow these cookies”.

In a disposable desktop Chrome profile in the UK, the updated extension clicked Cookies settings and Confirm my choices without clicking an acceptance control. The banner and preference center disappeared. The site's `OptanonConsent` changed from interaction count 0 to 1, retained `C0001:1` (strictly necessary), and recorded `C0003:0`, `C0002:0`, `C0004:0` (functional, performance, targeting). `OptanonAlertBoxClosed` was set. The refusal survived a reload with automation paused. Fourteen regression cases cover the compatibility path and its safety boundaries.

This live check applies to the observed English configuration and browser session. It does not prove that third-party cookies already present were erased, that every regional variant works, or that the site's network activity honors consent. Existing third-party cookies can remain; this extension records the site's available refusal choice rather than deleting cookies.

The repository's automated checks exercise syntax, rule shape, settings behavior and controlled DOM scenarios. These are regression checks for the implementation. Synthetic fixtures cannot prove the continuing behavior of hosted CMPs, which can vary with region, account settings, experiment assignment and vendor releases. Manual review should include fresh browser profiles, preference-center transitions, frames, open shadow roots, and confirmation that no acceptance action fires.

For a new provider rule, record the primary source, scope selectors to the visible provider UI, trace the actual handler, and add a controlled fixture containing both acceptance and refusal controls. Prefer an explicitly named refusal action. If the action is ambiguous, keep the interface untouched and retain the language fallback until there is stronger evidence.
